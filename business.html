Starting a Business in the USA

Your complete guide to American entrepreneurship

Why Start a Business in the USA?

  • ✅ Largest consumer market - 330 million potential customers
  • ✅ Access to capital - VCs, angel investors, business loans
  • ✅ Business-friendly environment - Easy incorporation, low barriers
  • ✅ Immigration pathway - E-2 visa or EB-5 options for foreign nationals
  • ✅ Innovation ecosystem - Silicon Valley, tech hubs, networking opportunities

Choose Your Business Structure

LLC (Most Popular)

Limited Liability Company - Best for most small businesses

Protection: Personal assets protected from business liabilities
Flexibility: Simple management, pass-through taxation
Cost: $50-500 to form (varies by state)
Best for: Small businesses, consultants, e-commerce

C-Corporation

Best for startups seeking venture capital funding

Funding: Can issue stock, attract investors easily
Scale: Ideal for high-growth companies
Complexity: More regulations, double taxation
Best for: Tech startups, companies seeking VC funding

Sole Proprietorship

Simplest form - you and your business are one legal entity

Simple: No formal registration required in most states
Low cost: Minimal startup costs
Risk: Personal assets at risk for business debts
Note: Not recommended for immigrants seeking visa sponsorship

Partnership

For businesses with multiple owners sharing responsibilities

Shared: Split profits, losses, and responsibilities
Types: General or Limited Partnership available
Important: Requires detailed partnership agreement
Best for: Professional services, family businesses

Step-by-Step: Forming Your LLC

1

Choose Your State

Delaware: Business-friendly laws, strong privacy protection
Wyoming: Low taxes, no state income tax, minimal reporting
Your operating state: Often simplest if you'll physically operate there

2

Name Your Business

Check name availability with your state's Secretary of State office. Name must include "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company". Avoid names too similar to existing businesses.

3

Appoint a Registered Agent

A US-based person or company to receive legal documents on behalf of your business. Services like Northwest Registered Agent or Incfile typically cost $100-200/year.

4

File Articles of Organization

Submit formation documents to your state's Secretary of State (filing fee: $50-500). Can usually be done online. Processing takes 1-2 weeks in most states.

5

Get Your EIN (Tax ID Number)

Apply for Employer Identification Number from IRS (free at IRS.gov). Required for opening business bank accounts, filing taxes, and hiring employees.

6

Create an Operating Agreement

Internal document outlining ownership percentages, management structure, and operating procedures. Not always legally required but highly recommended for protection.

7

Open a Business Bank Account

Keep personal and business finances separate. Major banks: Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo. Online options for international founders: Mercury, Brex, Wise Business.

8

Obtain Licenses & Permits

Requirements vary by industry, state, and locality. Check with your county clerk's office or use the SBA.gov license lookup tool to find what you need.

Important for E-2 Visa Applicants

If you're planning to use your business for an E-2 Treaty Investor visa (available to citizens of treaty countries), you'll need:

  • ✅ Substantial investment: Typically $100,000 - $200,000 minimum
  • ✅ Active business: Not just passive investment (real estate alone doesn't qualify)
  • ✅ Job creation: Must plan to hire US workers
  • ✅ Controlling interest: Own at least 50% of the business
  • ✅ Detailed business plan: Professional 3-5 year financial projections required
